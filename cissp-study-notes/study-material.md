* OSG (10/10) I read it at the beginning of my studying, cover-to-cover, to gain term recognition and to get an idea of the exam’s scope, returning at the very end to reinforce the specifics of processes such as RMF, Incident Response, BCP/DRP, SDLC, etc. If I had to study again, I would save using it until the end of my studies, because I ended up wasting a lot of time trying to memorize too many fine details.
* LearnZapp (10/10) This helped me identify and boost my weak areas. I ended up finishing with 88% readiness, 2097 questions answered since January. I mostly used 25-50 question custom tests, ignoring the flash cards and full practice tests. The scenario-based questions in particular helped me understand how to apply technical concepts from a managerial perspective.
* DestCert: Book (10/10), Questions App (9/10), MindMaps (8/10), and in-depth topic YT videos (Kerberos, Diffie-Hellman, etc.) (10/10) Not sure I could have passed the test with ONLY these DestCert resources, but they did an INCREDIBLE job of highlighting key concepts (accountability/responsibility, for example) and tying them all to form the big picture of cybersecurity management. The questions app can be buggy at times, but the content is at least as good as LearnZapp.
* CISSP for Dummies (7/10) I cherry-picked about 2/3 of it before I picked up the DestCert book, got hooked, and never went back. Not a bad book, but something about its layout didn’t match my desired study rhythm.
* 11th Hour CISSP (6/10) Not a comprehensive guide, but provides good summaries. The information contained is factually correct, but the overall content needs to be updated. The only real thing I recall learning well from this was the different types of fire suppression systems.
* Prabh Nair’s Coffee Shots (10/10) I wish I had watched more of these, as he covers a lot of material with his practice questions and their explanations. In particular, his IPSec, SAML, and Cryptography videos cleared a lot up for me.
* Pete Zerger Exam Cram and drill-down videos (8/10) These videos seemed to be a summary of the OSG, but not so detailed.
* Andrew’s 50 Questions (10/10) Watched it twice in three months, felt more optimistic about my performance after the second viewing. The thought process he communicates matches what you need for the real exam.
* How to Think Like a Manager (9/10) I wish it was longer than 25 practice scenarios, but it really helped me crack the code on what a manager thinks vs. a technician. I still don’t know what the difference between a 2-tier I and 2-tier II firewall architecture is though…
* Kelly’s Why You Will Pass the CISSP (10/10) I listened to this for the first time in February and every day this week, including on the way to the testing center today. Motivational and reassuring, to say the least.
* Thor’s Hard Practice Questions (7/10) These questions were SCARY, but the grueling experience of finishing all 125ish questions in one sitting prepared me well for test day. I don’t think I broke a 70% on any of these practice tests.
